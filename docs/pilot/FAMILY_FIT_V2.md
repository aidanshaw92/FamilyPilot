## What the proposed policy says

The current score is a weighted blend of five factors (age .25, accessibility .15, distance .15, budget .10, facilities .15). **The weights do not change.** What changes is what each factor is allowed to read.

| Question | Current | Proposed |
|---|---|---|
| Is a venue rewarded for how many facts were extracted about it? | Yes: facilities factor counts café, parking, free parking and baby changing for everyone; a confirmed yes adds, so a better-documented venue scores higher for every household | **No.** Only the facilities this household needs count: toilets (any child), baby changing (child under 4), anything it stated it must have. Café, free parking and playground never count until someone says they matter |
| Does a pushchair rating affect a household with no pushchair? | Yes: a "good" rating lifts everyone | **No.** Accessibility is read only for a household that uses a buggy (rating) or has a wheelchair or mobility-aid user (the venue's wheelchair claim). Otherwise the factor is left out of the blend |
| Is unknown treated as unsuitable? | In one place, yes: a must-have nobody has confirmed is called "Missing" and caps facilities at 35 | **No.** Unknown is neutral everywhere (75 age, 70 practical). Only a confirmed "no" for something the household said it always needs caps the factor and raises the "Missing" caution |
| What can raise the age factor? | Only a venue-wide recommended range (none of the ten has one) | A recommended range (96), or a reviewed **permanent provision** on the venue's own pages that covers the child's age (88). Never a category, marketing copy, or a programme on set days. A child the venue's own range excludes lowers it (42). Averaged over children |
| Can a confirmed conflict outrank a place without one? | Yes: the label says Poor, the order ignores it | **No.** A venue the planner would refuse for this household's non-negotiables is listed after every venue it would not refuse, whatever its score |

Six kinds of information, kept apart: **confirmed age-relevant activities** (range, provision) feed the age factor; **confirmed incompatibilities** (range excludes a child, a must-have confirmed absent, a pushchair or step-free restriction on the core visit, an age policy that excludes) feed the conflict group; **unknown suitability** is neutral and never listed as a conflict; **practical logistics** feed access and facilities and can refuse but never make a child "suited"; **household requirements** are the things it stated it must have; **general preferences** (budget, drive) keep their current factors.

## What I would be careful about before switching either part on

1. **The result still depends on how much age evidence has been reviewed, not only on the household.** Science Museum is #1 for the most households in the `after` state because it is the one pilot venue with reviewed, age-banded provision across the range (The Garden, 3 to 6; Wonderlab, 7 to 14). The RAF Museum, Natural History, Horniman, Mudchute and Gunnersbury have no reviewed age-specific provision, so they sit at the neutral 75. The age uplift is 88 against 75, worth roughly 3 to 4 points of the final score, where neighbours differ by 1 to 3. That is the intended effect of "confirmed age-relevant activity counts", and also the reason a venue nobody has read yet is at a disadvantage. It is a reason to widen reviewed coverage before the age factor is used for ranking, not a reason to loosen the evidence standard.
2. **A hard exclusion can be wrong when the data model is too coarse.** Household I (a child using a wheelchair, parking as a must-have) was refused six of the ten venues by the planner's own rule because each says "no parking on site". Four of them (Natural History Museum, Science Museum, Horniman, Discover) also publish Blue Badge spaces or bays, and the app holds no field for them. Under the hard-conflict policy a general "no parking" is now carried as *to check*, never as a refusal, for a party with a wheelchair or mobility-aid user (`parkingFor`, tested). The proper fix is a separate accessible-parking fact; this is the safeguard until it exists. It applies only under the flag; today's planner still refuses.
3. **Home and the planner used two definitions of a buggy conflict.** Home called a place "Probably not" for any child whose buggy was their only way round when a pushchair restriction covered the core visit; the planner refuses only a household that listed buggy access as a must-have, and otherwise warns with the venue's own words. They now agree on the planner's line. No pushchair rule is live in production (none is published), so no parent has seen the old wording.
4. **Not changed:** the weights; the budget and drive factors; the Excellent/Good/Possible labels; anything that depends on the day (a closure is shown beside the fit and enforced by the planner). The proposed ordering never *removes* a venue from a list; it lists a confirmed conflict after the others.
5. **Provider-only (unreviewed) venues** are untouched by the evidence-aware score: they stay capped and explained as unreviewed.

## How it is tested

- `family-fit-v2.test.ts` (32 tests): off means off; unknown is neutral and never a conflict; extra extracted facts the household did not ask for change nothing (and do today); a pushchair rating and a wheelchair claim speak only to households they matter to; age comes only from the venue's own range or a reviewed permanent provision, not from category, not from a programme, not past 90 days; a confirmed conflict is listed after a plain venue even with a higher score; ten cases in which the order lists, the badge and the planner's sequencer give the same answer; the Blue Badge safeguard.
