# ODbL assessment: what the architecture meets, what is still to build, what needs a judgment

14 October 2026. An engineer's reading of the primary texts, not legal advice. Sources, all fetched on 14 October from the publishers' own pages: the ODbL 1.0 legal code (opendatacommons.org); the OSM copyright page; the OSMF Community Guidelines for Collective Database (endorsed 2016-06-17), Horizontal Map Layers, Substantial, Produced Work and Trivial Transformations; the OSMF Attribution Guidelines and Licence and Legal FAQ; the OSMF Licensing Working Group page; the Open Government Licence v3.0; Wikidata:Licensing; the OS Open Greenspace documentation. It replaces the "send these six questions to counsel" framing of [ODBL_DECISION_MEMO.md](ODBL_DECISION_MEMO.md), which assumed a lawyer.

**The short version.** Two of the six open questions are avoidable by design, two are cheap to answer by complying conservatively, and two remain real judgments. None blocks the open-licence tranche (Wikidata CC0 and OS Open Greenspace OGL), which carries no ODbL obligation at all. Nothing OSM-derived is served until the build steps below are done and you have decided on the two judgments.

## What the proposed architecture is

OSM-derived entries live in one file, `server/places/data/identities/osm.json`, in the public repository. An entry carries a name, a category, a position, an https website and a review record: no OSM tag beyond those, no Google field (the closed entry shape refuses one, and a test proves it). A category is switched from Google's rows to the independent files as a whole (`cutover.json`), never blended with Google's rows within a type. Hours, facilities and ages stay "not yet reviewed" unless our own evidence layer, read from venues' own pages, says otherwise.

## The two real judgments

**A. The private alias join (row 9).** Saved places and plans on phones hold Google place IDs. To keep them working once the Google row is gone, the server maps that ID to the venue's entry. The Collective Database guideline says a join by key is a reference, and that data which references OSM may not be "independent", which could attach share-alike to the non-OSM side of the join. The Google side is the part nobody can relicense. ODbL 4.5(c) says internal use inside an organisation is not "to the public", and the map would be server-side and unpublished, but I cannot tell you that settles it.
*It is avoidable.* 67 of the 116 existing venues that have an independent path have one from OS or Wikidata as well as OSM, so their alias would point at an OGL or CC0 entry and raise no question. **45 venues continue only through OSM** (museums 18, parks 12, attractions 5, activities 4, farms 4, zoos 2). If you would rather not rely on the join, those 45 are served as new venues and anyone who saved one re-saves it. The choice is yours; the cost is 45 saved-place links, not 45 venues.

**B. The switch (row 7).** The weeks when a category has both Google rows and OSM entries look like a blend of OSM and non-OSM data for one type. The risk is the days of overlap, not the end state. *It is avoidable* by switching a category's Google rows off in the same deploy that switches its entries on: the registry and the cutover list change together, and the reader already serves a switched category from the independent files only. I cannot promise a regulator or a rights-holder would agree; I can say the overlap would be zero.

Two smaller points that are not judgments: whether a mobile app's Produced Work "offers" the database (4.6) is met conservatively by the Data licences screen linking to the file; and the OSMF's own guidance treats a Produced Work that is intended for extraction of the data as a database, so the catalogue endpoint is treated as part of the database, not as a map.

## Who could answer, without a lawyer

- **The licensor.** The OSMF Licensing Working Group's page invites "questions with respect to licensing, business models and similar subjects that you are asking the OSMF as licensor" at a `legal-questions` address on `osmfoundation.org` (the page renders it as `legal-questions` followed by `@osmfoundation.org`; check it on the page before sending). It says it will "try and respond as soon as possible" and that it "cannot provide legal advice or extensive business plan analysis". Free, authoritative on what the OSMF means by its own guidelines, and no guarantee of speed. The public `legal-talk` list is the other route; it is public.
- **A solicitor, for an hour.** If you want certainty on A and B, the two questions in this document are the whole brief. I have not appointed anyone and do not suggest a firm.
- **Neither.** Avoid A and B by design (above) and serve OSM entries on the conservative reading. The residual is a licensor who disagrees with the guidelines it has published, which I judge unlikely and cannot rule out.

## What I would send the OSMF, if you agree (one message, no company detail)

> We are building a small app that lists parks, museums and family attractions in London. We want to hold about 300 OpenStreetMap features (name, category, position, website) in a public JSON file under the ODbL, with the credit and a licence notice, and serve them in the app. Other categories come from other sources; we never mix OSM and non-OSM data in one feature type in the same region. (1) Do you agree that this is a Derivative Database that we satisfy by publishing the file with a notice? (2) Is a server-side, unpublished table mapping our own earlier identifiers to those entries a "reference" that affects the independence of the other dataset? (3) Is it enough, when we change a category from another source to OSM, to switch both in the same release?

I have not sent it and will not without your word.
